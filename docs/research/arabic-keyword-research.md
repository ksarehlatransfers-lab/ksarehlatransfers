# Arabic keyword research — Saudi Arabia

Target country: **Saudi Arabia**. Language: **Arabic**.

**This document was built independently of the English one.** The keywords
below are not translations of
[english-keyword-research.md](./english-keyword-research.md); several have no
English counterpart and the commercial vocabulary is different. Where a term
*is* the obvious equivalent, that is because the market uses it, not because it
was carried across.

## Data availability

Identical constraints to the English document: **no volume, no difficulty, no
CPC, no traffic, no rankings.** No keyword tool was available. Evidence is live
Arabic SERP composition plus competitor on-page observation.

One additional Arabic-specific limitation: the searches were run from a
US-located search environment. Arabic SERPs are **strongly** locale-sensitive,
more so than English ones, so the composition observed here may differ from
what a user in Riyadh sees. **Re-run every Arabic query from a Saudi IP or with
a Saudi locale parameter before relying on it.**

## The central finding: the market uses a different register from the competitor

Three Arabic searches were run. All three returned the same kind of result, and
it is not what the competitor is targeting.

| Query run | What the results were |
| --- | --- |
| `نقل خاص من الرياض إلى القصيم بريدة توصيل سيارة مع سائق` | **Entirely classified-ad listings** — mourjan.com (×6), mstaml.com, sabbar.com. Individual drivers advertising مشاوير خاصة. One listing quotes "التوصيل من بريدة إلى الرياض 400 ريال" |
| `توصيل من الرياض الى الطائف مشوار خاص سيارة` | **Entirely classifieds** — mstaml.com, mourjan.com (×7). Listings use توصيل مشاوير, مشاوير خاصة فورية وشهرية, سيارة عائلية 7 مقاعد |
| `سائق خاص بالساعة الرياض استئجار سيارة مع سائق يومي` | **OneClickDrive** (×8, a car-rental-with-driver marketplace) plus one job ad on sabbar.com |

### What this means

1. **The Arabic commercial SERP for intercity transfers has no professional
   operator on it at all.** It is individual drivers on classified-ad
   platforms. That is a larger, cleaner opening than anything on the English
   side.
2. **The working vocabulary is توصيل and مشوار / مشاوير**, not تاكسي and not
   شوفير. Real listings read "توصيل مشاوير داخل وخارج الرياض", "مشاوير خاصة
   فورية وشهرية", "مشاوير خاصة القصيم بريدة". These are the words Saudi users
   type and the words sellers use back.
3. **The competitor is in a different register entirely.** `/ar/` targets
   خدمة تاكسي, تاكسي VIP, شوفير تنفيذية, نقل VIP — a transliterated luxury
   vocabulary. They do not use توصيل or مشوار anywhere we observed. Either
   they are deliberately targeting a premium segment, or they are targeting
   translated English. Their Arabic page serving English content at
   `/ar/services/intercity/` suggests the latter.
4. **نقل خاص** — the term our own site already uses — sits in between: it is
   the formal, business-appropriate phrase, correct for a company and used in
   the classifieds' own section headings ("نقل وتوصيل"). It is the right
   **primary**. But توصيل and مشوار must appear as secondaries and in body
   copy, because that is the searcher's own language.

### Register map

| Term | Register | Who uses it | Our use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **نقل خاص** | Formal, commercial | Companies, section headings, our site | **Primary** on every page |
| **توصيل** | Everyday, transactional | Classified listings, real searchers | **Secondary**, in H2s and body |
| **مشوار / مشاوير** | Colloquial, Saudi-specific | Individual drivers, real searchers | **Secondary**, in FAQs and body |
| **سيارة مع سائق** | Descriptive, neutral | Rental marketplaces, searchers | **Secondary** |
| **تاكسي** | Transliteration | The competitor; street-hail connotation | Acknowledge once at most; not our product |
| **شوفير / ليموزين** | Luxury transliteration | The competitor | **Avoid** — implies a fleet class we have not confirmed |
| **سائق خاص** | Descriptive | Job ads *and* service ads — **ambiguous** | Use with care; see the warning below |

**Warning on سائق خاص.** Both Arabic searches that included it returned
**job advertisements** (sabbar.com, "سائق سيارة خاص — إعلان وظيفة") alongside
service listings. The phrase means both "a private driver [for hire]" and "a
private chauffeur [as a job]". A page targeting it will compete with
recruitment content. Use **استئجار سيارة مع سائق** or
**سائق خاص بالساعة** to disambiguate, and verify the SERP split before
committing.

### Two Arabic-specific decisions these raise

**1. Region vs city: القصيم or بريدة?**
The Arabic SERP we ran returned region-level pages
(`mourjan.com/sa/al-qassim/delivery-service/`) and listings naming
"القصيم بريدة عنيزة" together. Arabic local search appears to favour the
**region** name more than English does. Recommendation: keep the **city** in
the slug and the H1 (`بريدة`) for hreflang symmetry with the English URL, but
make **القصيم** a prominent secondary in the H2s, body and FAQs, and name
عنيزة and الرس in the practical sections. Confirm with a tool before relying on
it.

**2. Transliteration variants must be covered in body copy, not URLs.**
Arabic users type both العُلا and العلا (with and without the diacritic), and
both حائل and حايل. These are the *same* keyword and must never become separate
pages. Use the correct form in the H1 and metadata; let the variants appear
naturally in body copy and FAQ phrasing.

| كم سعر التوصيل من الرياض إلى `<مدينة>` | how much is the ride | Partially | Explain how the quotation is built; never a figure |

| أرخص توصيل | cheapest ride | **No** | Cannot substantiate; wrong positioning |

**Context that strengthens our position.** The one price we saw in the Arabic
SERP — "التوصيل من بريدة إلى الرياض 400 ريال" — is an individual's classified
ad with no verification, no vehicle specified and no booking process. Against
that, "a written quotation before anything is reserved, nothing added
afterwards" is a genuinely differentiated promise in Arabic, and it is true.

## 12. Validation checklist

- [ ] **Re-run every Arabic query above from a Saudi locale.** This is the most
      important item in this document; the observations here come from a
      US-located search.
- [ ] Measure the split between **توصيل**, **نقل**, **مشوار** and
      **سيارة مع سائق** with a real tool, per route. This decides H2 wording,
      not just metadata.
- [ ] Resolve **القصيم vs بريدة** and **المنطقة الشرقية vs الدمام** — region
      against city — on data.
- [ ] Confirm **سائق خاص** SERPs are service-led, not job-led, before using it
      as a primary anywhere.
- [ ] Check whether Arabic users search the **reverse** direction more than
      English users do (جدة إلى الرياض vs الرياض إلى جدة).
- [ ] Verify Arabic variant spellings (العُلا/العلا, حائل/حايل) resolve to one
      SERP, confirming they must stay one page.
- [ ] Check demand for **نقل عائلي** / family-group framing, which appeared
      repeatedly in listings ("سيارة عائلية 7 مقاعد") and may be a stronger
      Arabic angle than it is an English one.
