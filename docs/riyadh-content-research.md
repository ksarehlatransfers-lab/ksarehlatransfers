# Riyadh cluster — content research references

Every distance, duration and route fact published in the cluster traces to a
source listed here. Where sources disagree — and on these routes they
routinely do — the site publishes a **range** and says in a footnote why the
range exists, rather than picking the most flattering number.

the airport page was written. They were attempted and **both refused

So **nothing on the airport page is attributed to the airport itself.** That
is stated on the page where it matters — the departures panel and the

check-in rule, and send the reader to their airline instead.

If the owner can supply the airport's own published materials, or open those

### What was deliberately NOT published, and why

**No terminal map.** This is the significant one. The sources contradict each
other outright on which terminal serves which airline:

| | Saudipedia (Ministry of Media) | Wikipedia |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Terminals 1–2 | International, various carriers | T1 international for two low-cost carriers; T2 international for two others |
| Terminal 5 | **Domestic**, all airlines | **International**, all foreign carriers |

They cannot both be right, and trade press reports terminals being closed and
reopened, so assignments have demonstrably moved. A wrong chart on a transfer
site does not merely misinform — it sends a passenger to the wrong building at
an airport with five of them. **The page publishes no chart and no numbered
terminal at all**, and answers "which terminal" from the flight number, which
is both the honest answer and the better service. A FAQ states the
disagreement in plain language rather than hiding the gap.

**No list of inclusions.** Waiting time, meet-and-greet detail, tolls and
child seats are operational facts nobody has confirmed — the same class of
information that still blocks the private-driver-hire page. The "what your
quotation covers" section therefore describes the **process** (a written price
naming the vehicle, terminal, meeting arrangement and total, with nothing
added afterwards) and lists what it will not claim. That was a deliberate
substitution for the planned "what's included" block.

**No check-in guidance.** No readable airport or operator source states one.

**No fares, frequencies or metro ticket prices.**

## Standing rule

Nothing on these pages claims a price, a guaranteed journey time, a specific
vehicle, a fleet, a licence, a customer count, a rating or a review. None of
those are established for this business. Figures that *are* published are
labelled approximate in the interface, not only here.

## Deliberate omissions

- **No prices anywhere.** Several operator pages found during research quote
  fixed fares. None is ours and none is repeated.
- **No rail timetables or fares.** The Dammam page compares road and rail on
  *suitability* and sends the reader to the operator for anything numeric,
  because schedules change and a stale timetable on a transfer site is worse
  than none.
- **No claim about crossing the causeway.** The Al Khobar page says we can
  reach the causeway, that crossing depends on rules and vehicle permissions
  in force at the time, and that visas and border formalities are the
  traveller's own. It does not say we take you to Bahrain.
- **No Hajj or Umrah positioning** anywhere in this cluster. Makkah and
  Madinah are treated as ordinary intercity destinations for this phase, as
  the specification requires, and neither has a page yet.
- **No driver-hours promise.** The Jeddah FAQ answers "can one driver cover
  the whole journey?" with "that depends and we confirm it when quoting"
  rather than a reassurance we cannot back.

## Needs verification before these pages are promoted

1. **Afif's position on the route (~480 km).** The only source placing Afif on
   Highway 40 between Taif and Riyadh is a weather-radar vendor page with no
   official backing. The town is on the corridor, but the distance figure
   should be checked against an authoritative road atlas or the Ministry of
   Transport before it is relied on.
2. **Mandatory rest-area spacing.** A Wikipedia mirror references a rule of
   roughly 60 minutes of travel between rest areas, sourced to a 2014 news
   article. This could not be verified against an official source and is
   therefore **not stated anywhere on the site** — the pages describe where
   people usually stop, not where they are required to.
3. **Exact endpoints used for every distance figure.** Aggregators measure
   city centre to city centre. Real journeys are address to address, which is
   why every figure is given with a tilde and a range.
4. **Official references the client supplied** — King Khalid International
   Airport, the SAR station directory, the SAR railway network and the Riyadh
   public transport network — have **not yet been consulted**. They are not
   needed for batch 1, which contains no airport or railway page, but they are
   the primary sources for batches 3 and 4 and should be read before those
   pages are written rather than after.
